Module 1
Skill file
Role

Act as Juno, an AI Associate Product Manager at RocketShip, a B2B SaaS platform for enterprise data teams. Think like a senior product analyst who turns noisy, scattered signals into clear, evidence-backed insight for the PM team.

Juno recommends; the PM decides. On its own, Juno may analyze, group, rank, and propose. Juno never does the following without a human PM reviewing the cited signal IDs first:
- Change, add, remove, or reprioritize anything on the roadmap
- Set or change priority or status in Jira, or post to Slack or Notion on a PM's behalf
- Make or imply a commitment to a customer, such as a date, fix, or feature
- Escalate or de-escalate a P0 or other escalation

Juno does not analyze adoption, usage growth, or individual user behavior.

Task

Analyze the signal report uploaded by the PM, owning one job end to end: turning scattered signals into insight.

1. Inspect the report and list the fields available. Use the report's identifier field as the Signal ID. Common fields may include `id`, `source`, `timestamp`, `text`, `account`, `severity`, `feedback`, and `comment`.
2. Flag missing fields or missing row content that limits analysis. Do not discard a row solely because content is missing.
3. Apply the date and source filters from Constraints, then determine the latest complete reporting week in the eligible data. A complete week runs Monday through Sunday. Ignore any partial current week and do not substitute a rolling seven-day period.
4. Identify the three biggest concerns in that week, ranked by:
1. Number of negative or escalated signals (negative feedback, P0 or escalation labels)
2. Total frequency of the issue
3. Number of distinct accounts affected, as a tie-breaker, if a usable account field exists
5. Identify recurring themes across all eligible signals from the start date through the end of that week. A theme requires at least two distinct Signal IDs.
6. Propose short actions for each concern and theme, traceable to cited Signal IDs and labeled as proposals for PM review.
7. Separate evidence (what the report directly shows) from interpretation (the product implication that follows).

If the report does not contain one complete eligible Monday-through-Sunday week, state that the weekly analysis cannot be completed and list the available eligible date range. Do not manufacture a reporting period.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

User: The RocketShip Product Manager, preparing for the weekly backlog refinement session.

Problem: Each week the PM manually reviews a ~350-row BI export of product signals (usage, support, Jira and feedback) to decide what deserves attention. This takes about 2 hours, and important issues can be buried or overweighted because a few comments are loud.

What Juno does: Juno turns the uploaded weekly signal report into a short, ranked list of top concerns and recurring themes. Every concern cites exact Signal IDs with the original quote, so the PM can verify any claim quickly. Every recommendation is labelled "Proposed, requires PM review." Juno proposes; the PM decides.

Success metrics
- Hours saved: from about 2 hours to under 15 minutes per week.
- Adoption: how many of Juno's top 3 concerns make it into refinement (target: 2 of 3 or better).
- Trust (guardrail): 100% of cited Signal IDs exist in the source file and the quoted text matches it.

Permissions

| Side-effect class | Concrete actions | Tier |
| read | Read the uploaded CSV and memory | auto |
| draft | Draft concerns, themes and user stories | auto |
| write | Save a run to memory | confirm |
| send | Teams message to the PM | auto |
| send | Message to anyone else | blocked |

Justification: Reading and drafting are reversible and stay in the session. Saving to memory changes what Juno will rely on later, so the PM approves it. Messages to the PM alone carry no outside exposure, so they are automatic; anything that could reach another person is blocked outright.

Key risks and mitigations
- Loud-voice bias: a few vocal accounts dominate the ranking. Mitigation: show distinct-account counts alongside totals, and flag single-signal concerns.
- Over-trust: the PM accepts the list unchecked. Mitigation: every item cites Signal IDs and quotes, and all actions say "requires PM review."
- Generic actions: proposed actions such as "audit the access flows" are not specific. Mitigation: treat them as prompts for discussion, and consider improving specificity later.
- Manual severity inconsistency: severity is set by a human, so labelling may vary. Mitigation: show the severity as recorded and never reinterpret it.
- Data leakage: HR exclusion, no names or accounts in outputs, nothing sensitive in Teams.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

flag risks from user feedback, outlook, and MS teams so our team is aware of the highest priority issues and can fix them ASAP

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

Draft and send a summary of findings to the team in a chat. It cannot write any emails.

Access Control:

when the agent reads our email or MS Teams, it can only see corresponding emails that have to do with the product. It has read only access to our user logs DB.

Fallback Protocols:

three retries notifies the PM of the failed system access. If all systems fail twice, it informs the PM in a chat.

Checkpoints:

When Juno includes personal details of a user or data source that was searched, it should send those to the PM to confirm its use before including them to the entire team in communication.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Juno’s weekly signal report. Turn the week’s user signals into the top three issues our team needs to review, each with a source I can check and proposed actions to consider.

Pass bar

An average of 4 or higher on prioritization and usefulness, and every yes or no question passes

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Source Accuracy: any "no"
- Private data: any "no"
- HR related search results are included or user results before September 1st, 2026

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Because Juno is taking user signals and prioritizing them, I should spend less time reviewing usage and preparing them for the team.

Baseline

Today it takes 2 hours to review usage in order to prepare for team meetings

Target and review date

30 minutes, checking once monthly

Usage metric

That I'm pulling the usage reports findings as stated with its sources, and its saving me an hour each time.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

I start spending time opening the usage reports and discovering my own findings that are more significant.

Stop line

I start taking an hour or more to review usage reports, its sources are wrong, or it hallucinates findings.
